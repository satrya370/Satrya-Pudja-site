Most articles are written to be read. This one is written to survive being cut down to a single quoted sentence.

GEO here stands for Generative Engine Optimization — not location targeting. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overview rarely read an article start to finish; they lift out one section, one sentence, sometimes a single claim, and cite that. This pipeline researches a topic, drafts a full article, and scores every section against a 7-category rubric built around exactly that behavior — then rewrites whatever would get skipped over, and publishes only once the draft actually clears the bar.

How it works

Research, then outline, then draft

A researcher role gathers sources on the topic, an outline role structures the piece, and a writer role drafts the full article — three separate passes instead of one prompt trying to do everything at once.

Scored on two tracks at once

A zero-token rule-based checker scans for things like filler openings and sourced numeric claims, while a separate AI critic judges harder-to-automate things like whether each section actually answers its own heading. The two scores combine into one GEO score out of 100.

The score decides what happens next

Below 60, the draft is rejected outright. Between 60 and 79, it goes back for revision — capped at 2 automatic passes so a weak draft can't loop forever. At 80 or above, it moves on to publish.

Metadata, then a live publish

Schema and metadata are generated, then the article is posted directly to WordPress — via the WordPress.com REST API, since the target blog is WordPress.com-hosted rather than self-hosted. A result notification with the score and the live link goes out either way.

What the GEO score actually checks

Not an official industry metric — there's no standard "GEO score" from OpenAI, Google, or Perplexity. This is a heuristic proxy for the structural traits that make a section easier for a retrieval system to extract, weighted across 7 categories.

20 pts

Answer-first structure

Every H2 has to open with a self-contained 40–80 word answer to its own heading before any elaboration — the exact habit answer engines reward when they extract the first sentences of a section.

20 pts

Citation & fact density

Numeric claims need a traceable source — a link or a named attribution — checked automatically so unsourced statistics don't slip into a published article.

15 pts

Structure & formatting

Headings, lists, and scannability — the formatting cues that make a section easy to isolate and lift out of a longer document.

15 pts

Self-containment & entity clarity

Can a paragraph be understood on its own, pulled out of context, without needing the three paragraphs before it to make sense?

15 pts

Language & tone

Filler openings and hedging language — "in today's fast-evolving world," "it cannot be denied" — are penalized directly rather than left to editorial taste.

10 pts

Metadata & schema signals

Structured JSON-LD is generated and embedded in the published post — a machine-readable signal about what the article actually is, alongside the human-readable text.
