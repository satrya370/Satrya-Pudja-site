Product strategy for a six-template wedding invitation platform trying to grow past its first customers.

From December 2025 to February 2026 I worked as product manager for HariBersama.com, an Indonesian digital invitation platform. My scope: find the partnerships that could expand its reach, stress-test its business model with structured strategy frameworks, and turn customer and market research into concrete feature and social media direction.

What HariBersama is

HariBersama is a digital invitation platform built for the Indonesian market — couples and event hosts pick from premium templates, personalize them, and share a single link instead of printing and distributing physical cards. At the time I worked on it, the platform offered six templates spanning traditional Balinese to contemporary "glass modern" aesthetics: Niratha, Wiswambara, Anggara, Saraswati, Prameswari, and Putri.

The product itself is already feature-rich for a wedding-specific use case: countdown timers, date reminders, embedded location maps, ambient music, photo galleries, a couple's love-story section, guest RSVP confirmation, and gift-registry sharing. The strategic question wasn't "does the product work" — it was "how does this reach more of the customers who'd actually want it."

What I actually did

B2B partnership research

Identified the categories of business that touch a couple's wedding planning journey before they ever search for an invitation — wedding organizers, venues, vendors — and mapped which of those relationships could realistically become a distribution channel rather than a one-off referral.

SWOT & business-model evaluation

Ran the platform's position through SWOT analysis and business-model frameworks — not as a formality, but to force an honest answer on where the product's real strengths were defensible versus where competitors with more templates or lower prices could erode it.

Social media marketing strategy

Defined target audience segments, content direction, and channel positioning — deciding what HariBersama should actually be posting and where, instead of generic "post consistently" advice.

Product development support

Researched what customers and the broader market actually needed, evaluated which potential features were worth building against that need, and turned it into concrete recommendations for the product and service roadmap.

Why this combination of work

  • Partnerships and strategy have to agree with each other. A partnership channel that contradicts what the SWOT says the platform is actually good at just adds distribution to a weak position — so the two workstreams were run together, not handed off in sequence.
  • Social strategy follows from positioning, not the other way around. Deciding content direction before the business-model analysis was done would mean marketing whatever seemed appealing, not what the platform could actually defend against competitors.
  • Feature recommendations are only useful if they're traceable to a real need. Every product suggestion I made was tied back to something specific found in customer or market research — not a feature that seemed like a good idea in isolation.
