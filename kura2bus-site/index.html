Why 97 people clicked "book now" and only 1 completed the booking.

My bachelor thesis analyzed the "Highlight of Central Bali" tour product page on kura2bus.com using Google Analytics 4 — the same site and page I manage as part of my SEO and web analytics work. This page summarizes the real findings: what drives traffic to the page, how differently paid and organic visitors behave, and where the booking funnel actually breaks.

What this page is

"Highlight of Central Bali" is one of PT JTB Indonesia's tour products, sold through kura2bus.com. As the person managing SEO and analytics for the site, I used it as the subject of my final-year thesis: a diagnostic study of why a page that clearly attracts interest was converting almost none of that interest into completed bookings.

The research combined qualitative data — observation and stakeholder interviews on the company's existing optimization strategy — with quantitative data from GA4 Explore, examined across four dimensions: traffic trends, geographic distribution, traffic sources, and on-page user interaction (page views, engagement, scroll depth, booking-button clicks).

What the data actually showed

Traffic acquisition

Growth tracks the ad budget, not organic pull

Traffic spikes line up with active paid campaigns and drop when promotion stops — the page hasn't built a stable organic acquisition base yet.

User behavior

Organic visitors read 5.5× longer than paid visitors

Paid traffic: ~70 second average session, 0.63 engagement rate — decent, but shallow. Organic traffic: ~388 second average session — visitors who found the page searching are exploring it in real depth.

Channel anomaly

Google/CPC: ~148 visits, near-zero interaction, zero bookings

A meaningful chunk of paid traffic converts to almost no engagement and no booking action at all — a mismatch between the ad's keywords/messaging and what users find on landing.

Geography

The Japan-targeted page isn't reaching Japan

Audience is still domestic-dominated. A localized page was already built for the Japanese market, but it shows no meaningful engagement in the main traffic reports for the study period.

Conversion

97 booking clicks. 1 completed transaction.

This is the core finding. The page succeeds at generating booking intent — the problem is entirely downstream, in whatever happens after a visitor clicks "book."

Overall

The gap isn't attention — it's the handoff

Traffic acquisition and content relevance are working reasonably well. What's missing is synchronization between acquisition, on-page experience, and the booking system's readiness to actually close the sale.

What I recommended

  • Rework Google/CPC targeting for international markets like Japan — shift budget toward more specific, localized keywords that match actual search intent in the target country, and localize ad creative to language and culture instead of relying on a translated landing page alone.
  • Double down on organic, deliberately — since organic visitors already show far deeper engagement, invest in search-authority-building content updates rather than treating organic as an afterthought to paid.
  • Fix the booking flow, not the landing page — run usability testing specifically on the reservation form and payment process to find where users actually drop off between the 97 clicks and the 1 transaction; simplify the steps and make cost information transparent earlier.
  • Turn GA4 into a weekly habit, not a one-off report — regular review of engagement and event metrics so channel-level anomalies (like the CPC gap found here) get caught and corrected before more ad budget is spent on them.

Method, briefly

Qualitative: stakeholder interviews

Observation and interviews with the company to understand the existing SEO, content, advertising, and digital marketing strategy behind the page.

Quantitative: GA4 Explore

Traffic trend, geographic distribution, source/medium, and event-level interaction data (page view, engagement, scroll depth, booking-button clicks) pulled directly from Google Analytics 4.

Descriptive–diagnostic analysis

Sequential exploratory design — qualitative context first, then quantitative data interpreted against that context, not in isolation.
